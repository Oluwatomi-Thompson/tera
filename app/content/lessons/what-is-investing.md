## Content

If you have a mobile money wallet, you're already "financially included" — and
in Rwanda, most people are. Mobile money reaches roughly 86% of adults
nationally, part of a 96% overall financial inclusion rate (NISR, 2024).

But financial inclusion isn't the same as investing.

**Saving** means putting
money aside, in a mobile money wallet, a bank account, or through an informal means — and getting back roughly what you put in. 
If you keep **50,000 RWF** tucked away in a mobile money wallet for a year, you will still have 50,000 RWF at the end of it.
**Investing** means putting money into something (shares in a company, a
government bond, a professionally managed fund) with the expectation that it
grows in value over time — and the real possibility that it could also lose
value. That's the trade-off, more potential growth for more risk, is the core idea
behind every lesson in Tera.

Nationally, more than 90% of Rwandan adults already use a formal financial service (NISR, 2024). 
Yet a 2026 study of 300 young Rwandan adults (ages 18–32) found that participants showed only modest knowledge of core financial concepts, with the gap wider among women and less-educated respondents (Ntakirutimana et al., 2026). 
In other words: access to financial tools is not the problem. Knowing what to do with them is. That's the gap Tera exists to close.


**Why this matters now:** money sitting still loses it's value every year to
inflation, meaning **50,000 RWF** buys less at the market next year than it does today.
Money invested, even at a modest rate, has a chance to grow faster than
that. The rest of this course walks through how, specifically, that works in
Rwanda — not in a US brokerage account you'll never open.
